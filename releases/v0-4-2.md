# CGP v0.4.2

The extensible data types release: the point at which CGP stopped merely *reading* fields from a
struct and learned to construct them, and gained the same generic access to an enum's variants. It is
the origin of the extensible builder and extensible visitor patterns, and of safe upcasting and
downcasting between enums.

## What it introduced

**Extensible records** give a struct a *partial* form: a generated companion type parameterized by
one presence marker per field, where `IsPresent` maps a field to its own type and `IsNothing` maps it
to `()`. `HasBuilder` produces the all-absent starting value, `BuildField` fills one field and returns
a builder whose *type* records the change, and `FinalizeBuild` converts an all-present partial record
into the real struct. Running in reverse, `IntoBuilder` and `TakeField` let fields be moved *out* of a
struct, which is what makes `CanBuildFrom` able to merge one struct into a larger one field by field.

**Extensible variants** are the categorical dual, built from the same parts inverted. `FromVariant`
constructs an enum from a tagged value, `HasExtractor` converts an enum into its partial form, and
`ExtractField` removes one variant and returns `Result<Value, Remainder>`. Absence here is not `()`
but the uninhabited `Void`, marked by `IsVoid` — which produces the release's most striking result:
extracting every variant in turn narrows the remainder to a type that cannot be constructed, so the
compiler accepts the match as exhaustive with no `unreachable!()` and no runtime assertion.
`FinalizeExtract` generalizes that discharge.

**Casting** falls out of both. `CanUpcast` lifts a value into an enum with a superset of its variants
and `CanDowncast` narrows into a subset, returning the unmatched remainder — the same
`FieldsExtractor` machinery, differing only in which side supplies the field list. Neither enum needs
to know about the other.

**The dispatchers** turn all of this into usable providers. `BuildWithHandlers` initializes an empty
partial record, pipes it through a list of handlers, and finalizes; `MatchWithHandlers` does the
variant-side equivalent. Reference variants of the handler traits arrive so that a visitor can borrow
rather than consume its input.

One preset improvement rides along: a direct delegation mode used in preset inheritance. Two bug
fixes matter for correctness — `HasField<Index<{i}>>` is derived for tuple structs, and unsized
generic arguments are allowed in `IsProviderFor`.

## What it changed or removed

Nothing was removed. The release is almost entirely additive; the derive surface it introduced
(`HasFields`, `BuildField`, `FromVariant`, `ExtractField`) had to be applied a derive at a time, which
is the main thing later releases changed.

## How it relates to v0.8.0

**Current in full.** [Extensible records](../cgp/concepts/extensible-records.md) and
[extensible variants](../cgp/concepts/extensible-variants.md) are documented as concepts, with
[dispatching](../cgp/concepts/dispatching.md) covering the builder and visitor dispatchers. Every
trait this release introduced exists: [`HasBuilder`](../cgp/reference/traits/has_builder.md),
[`ExtractField`](../cgp/reference/traits/extract_field.md),
[`FromVariant`](../cgp/reference/traits/from_variant.md),
[`HasFields`](../cgp/reference/traits/has_fields.md),
[`MapType`](../cgp/reference/traits/map_type.md), and
[`CanUpcast` / `CanDowncast` / `CanBuildFrom`](../cgp/reference/traits/cast.md). The two worked
scenarios from the announcement are re-derived in current syntax as the
[application builder](../examples/application-builder.md) and
[expression interpreter](../examples/expression-interpreter.md) examples.

**Renamed.** The generated partial types gained a `__Partial` prefix in [v0.5.0](v0-5-0.md), so
`PartialPerson` is now `__PartialPerson` — every partial type name in the four-part series is missing
it.

**Restructured.** `BuildField` stopped being a primitive in [v0.5.0](v0-5-0.md), which introduced
`UpdateField` and made `BuildField` a blanket impl over it. The individual derives were subsumed by
the umbrella [`#[derive(CgpData)]`](../cgp/reference/derives/derive_cgp_data.md) in the same release,
though the individual ones still work.

**Its future work shipped.** The announcement lists filling missing fields with defaults, overriding
existing fields, dropping fields during a merge, additional arguments to visitors, `&mut` visitors,
and a macro to remove the boilerplate of implementing a plain trait through a dispatcher. Almost all
of that arrived in [v0.5.0](v0-5-0.md): `finalize_with_default`, the `IsOptional` marker and the
[optional-field builder](../cgp/reference/traits/optional_fields.md), the mutable and tuple-input
matchers, and [`#[cgp_auto_dispatch]`](../cgp/reference/macros/cgp_auto_dispatch.md) — proposed in
part 4 under the name `#[cgp_dispatch]`.

**The unwritten fifth part is still unwritten.** Part 4 promised a piece on the full computation
hierarchy — `Computer`, `ComputerRef`, `TryComputer`, `Handler`, the promotion adapters, and
`PipeHandlers` versus `PipeMonadic`. It has never been published, which leaves
[handlers](../cgp/concepts/handlers.md) and [monadic handlers](../cgp/concepts/monadic-handlers.md) as
the only accounts of that material and marks a real gap in the site's coverage.
